Frequently Asked Questions

Key civil engineering insights regarding IS 10262:2019 concrete mix proportioning.

The 2019 revision introduced several critical updates:
  • Target mean strength equation now considers factor X ($f'_{ck} = f_{ck} + X$) alongside $f_{ck} + 1.65S$.
  • Water-cement ratio graphs (Figure 1) categorized into 3 curves: Curve 1 (OPC 33/PPC), Curve 2 (OPC 43), and Curve 3 (OPC 53).
  • Air content estimation (Table 3) integrated explicitly for nominal maximum sizes of aggregate (10, 20, and 40 mm).
  • Expanded provisions for self-compacting concrete, high-strength concrete, and mass concrete.
Per Clause 4.2 of IS 10262:2019, target compressive strength at 28 days is the maximum of two governing formulas:
f'ck = max( fck + 1.65 × S , fck + X )
Where S is the standard deviation from Table 2 and X is the factor from Table 1. This prevents under-designing when standard deviation is either low or untested.
Table 5 proportions coarse aggregate based on a benchmark water-cement ratio of 0.50. For every ±0.05 change in the adopted water-cement ratio, the coarse aggregate volume fraction is adjusted by ∓0.01. Lower w/c mixtures have richer paste, allowing a higher volume of coarse aggregate without risking segregation.
Table 5 mandates minimum concrete grades for reinforced concrete: Mild (M20), Moderate (M25), Severe (M30), Very Severe (M35), and Extreme (M40). If an engineer selects M20 for a Severe exposure condition, our engine flags it with an INVALID GRADE ❌ badge because IS 456 requires minimum M30 to prevent chloride-induced rebar corrosion.
Theoretical calculations assume aggregates in Saturated Surface Dry (SSD) state. On a live construction site, aggregates often carry free surface water or dry absorption voids. If free moisture is positive, that water enters the mix, increasing the effective w/c ratio and causing strength loss. Our calculator deducts aggregate free water directly from the batch mixing water.
